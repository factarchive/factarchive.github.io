Demographic and Social Trends

Europe – population share

Pew Research Center (2017) projected the Muslim share of the EU+ population could reach 7.4% (zero migration), 11.2% (medium), or 14% (high migration) by 2050. Higher fertility and continued immigration are the primary drivers. National offices (Eurostat, Statistics Sweden, INSEE) supply the base birth and net-migration series.

Europe – attitudes toward Sharia

Multiple surveys record substantial support for ranking Sharia above national law. A 2025 French poll found 57% of Muslims aged 15–24 said Sharia should outrank French law on certain matters; overall figures near 46% have been reported. Earlier multi-country work (Koopmans et al.) found averages around 65% of Muslim respondents stating Sharia is more important than host-country law.

United States

Pew estimates place the U.S. Muslim population near 1% in the early 2020s. Growth continues via immigration and above-average fertility. American Muslims vote heavily Democratic (commonly 70–80%+ in recent cycles). Absolute numbers remain far below European levels; local concentrations already affect some districts.

Global baseline (Pew 2013)

In the 39-country survey, majorities in South Asia, the Middle East-North Africa, Southeast Asia, and sub-Saharan Africa favored making Sharia the official law (examples: 99% Afghanistan, 91% Iraq, 84% Pakistan, 72% Indonesia). Support was markedly lower in Central Asia and parts of Southern/Eastern Europe.

Minority tipping dynamics

A 2018 Science study found a committed minority of approximately 25% could overturn established group norms in controlled experiments. Below that threshold influence remained minimal. Concentrated local populations can shift school boards, councils, and neighborhood norms at lower national percentages.
